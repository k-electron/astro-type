# Accuracy and speed benchmarks for beginner touch typists

Research for [#127](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/issues/127), under the map [#123](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/issues/123) ("Honest Stars"). Written 2026-11-17.

**Question:** what accuracy and speed do established typing tutors and research expect of beginner touch typists, stage by stage, so the Star bars can be tuned on evidence (P12)?

**How much to trust each source:**

- **Primary:** a tutor's own help pages or source code (keybr.com, Monkeytype and GNU Typist are open source, so their source code is quoted where their help pages say nothing); peer-reviewed papers read in full (Arif & Stuerzlinger 2009, Soukoreff & MacKenzie 2003, Dhakal et al. 2018, Pinet et al. 2022); a US federal job posting.
- **Weaker, and marked where used:** keybr's own success stories (self-selected users), and figures that Pinet et al. cite from older papers that I couldn't read myself.
- **Not used:** blogs, typing-test sites that don't teach, and any secondary page I couldn't trace back to a source.

Each section keeps **what the sources say** apart from **inference** (my reading, marked _Inference_). Figures about astro-type itself come from this repo (`src/curriculum/curriculum.ts`, `src/game/rules.ts`) at commit `b3450c9`.

---

## Bottom line

1. **Tutors' accuracy bars cluster at 90, 95 and 97%, and the top reward is always "no errors at all".** Typing.com's default Stars are 2 Stars at 90% and 3 Stars at 95% (1 Star just for finishing). GNU Typist makes a drill repeat if errors exceed 3% (so 97% to pass). TypingClub's accuracy goal is always 100%, and Ratatype gives a separate reward for an exercise "without errors". No tutor I found publishes a passing bar below 90%.
2. **Tutors don't agree on what accuracy counts.** keybr counts each character of the text once: it is an error if any wrong key was pressed before the right one. That is a first-attempt measure. TypingClub reports both a corrected "Accuracy" and a "Real Accuracy" (the share of the text typed without any mistake). Monkeytype counts every key press, and so does Typing.com as far as its help says.
3. **Everyone who documents the clock starts it on the first keystroke and stops it on the last.** That includes the text-entry research standard (which, for that reason, doesn't count the first character), keybr, Monkeytype, GNU Typist, and the 136-million-keystroke study. None of them counts time before the first key. Only Monkeytype deals with idle gaps: it counts seconds with no keypress as AFK time, and it voids a test whose last 5 seconds had no keypress.
4. **There is no single "net WPM".** Typing.com reports gross WPM with no penalty for errors. GNU Typist subtracts one whole word (5 characters) per error. Monkeytype counts only correctly typed words. Typesy multiplies speed by accuracy. astro-type subtracts one character per wrong keystroke, which is a much milder penalty than GNU Typist's.
5. **Speeds after about a month:** I found no open, controlled study that follows beginners through a month of daily touch-typing practice. The nearest evidence:
   - keybr's own examples go from under 20 to 40 WPM in 5.5 hours over 11 days, and from 20 to 45 WPM in about 10 hours over 22 days. These are self-selected users.
   - keybr's default target speed for unlocking the next letter is 35 WPM.
   - In a sample of 168,000 adults, the slowest tenth typed under about 26 WPM (mean 20.9), and the average was 52 WPM.
   - A US federal clerical job asks for 40 WPM with 3 or fewer errors in 5 minutes.

   _Inference:_ 20–25 WPM at 95% after about 8–10 hours of play is a modest, reachable goal. Many of today's 2-Star and 3-Star WPM bars sit above it.
6. **A minimum run of clean keystrokes is not a pass condition in any tutor I found.** The nearest things are:
   - zero-error rewards (Ratatype's target, TypingClub's 100% goal for all five stars);
   - "fail on the first wrong key" modes (Monkeytype's Master difficulty, a drill on Keyboarding Online with 0 errors allowed);
   - GNU Typist's drills, which don't allow Backspace.

   Where the documentation gives a purpose, it is putting accuracy ahead of speed. Streaks in the tutors I checked count practice days or lessons, not keystrokes.

**Planning benchmarks:** Typing.com also publishes benchmark bands for different learner groups, which it calls planning guidance, not built-in targets ([Benchmarks by group](https://support.typing.com/en/articles/9045953)). Its accuracy bands run 85%+, 90%+, 95%+ and 98%+. Its top band is 40+ WPM at 98%+, and the band below that is 30–40 WPM at 95%+. The groups don't map onto this game's players, so treat these as a scale, not as targets.

**Research measures:** text-entry research separates errors that are **corrected** (wrong keys later erased) from errors **left in the final text**. Soukoreff & MacKenzie's _total error rate_ adds both and divides by all keys typed except editing keys. Their point is that "putting effort into correcting errors, transfers keystrokes from INF to IF, but does not affect the total error rate", so the measure doesn't depend on whether the typist corrects ([CHI 2003](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/chi03.html)). Arif & Stuerzlinger show that how errors are handled changes every common error metric ([TIC-STH 2009](https://www.asarif.com/pub/Arif_TIC-STH2009.pdf)).

**How accurate real typists are:**

- **Dhakal et al. 2018** (168,000 volunteers on a transcription test, free to correct):
  - mean uncorrected error rate 1.17%;
  - Backspace or Delete made up 6.31% of keypresses;
  - for the slowest tenth (mean 20.9 WPM), those figures were 1.78% and 9.05%

  ([CHI 2018 paper](https://userinterfaces.aalto.fi/136Mkeystrokes/resources/chi-18-analysis.pdf), results table).
- **Pinet et al. 2022** (1,301 university students):
  - errors including corrections were "around 12% for the most proficient and 20% for the least proficient typists";
  - their measure also counts the Backspace keys, and they say it "might not be directly comparable to other studies";
  - they cite older typewriter studies, which they summarise as typically under 3.2% for experts and novices (Grudin 1983, Salthouse 1986). I didn't read those two papers myself.

  ([Cognitive Research 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-022-00424-3), full text via [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC9356123)).

### Inference

- **The 90 / 95 / 97% steps are the tutors' own steps.** P2's floor of no Star below 90% matches Typing.com's 2-Star default. 95% matches Typing.com's 3-Star default and the game's success criteria. 97% is GNU Typist's pass mark. A 3-Star bar of 97–99% is as strict as anything a tutor uses as a pass mark, and only the "no errors" rewards go further.
- **What counts as an error matters as much as the number.** In astro-type a wrong key is a Miss and the Drone stays, so the player presses again; there is no Backspace. P10's "first attempts" is the keybr and TypingClub "Real Accuracy" measure: one error per character at most, however many wrong keys were pressed. Today's rule (`correct / total keystrokes`) charges every wrong press, as Monkeytype does. Switching to first attempts raises the measured accuracy of a player who hits several wrong keys in a row on one character, so a bar set under today's measure becomes a little easier under the new one.
- **In free typing where people correct, every-keystroke accuracy is well below the final-text figures.** Average typists pressed Backspace on about 6% of keypresses, and slow ones on about 9%. Most corrections follow at least one wrong key, so their wrong-key rate is probably a few percent or more. This is only a rough comparison: Dhakal et al. let people retype whole stretches, and sentences aren't drills. Still, it suggests a 97–99% first-attempt bar is demanding for someone typing at 20–30 WPM. It is not a beginner's floor.

### Inference

- **Starting the clock on the first keystroke is universal among the sources that document it.** astro-type counts Run Time from launch, which is the Known departure P10 names. On a Stage of 56–196 characters (see §5), the seconds before the first keystroke are a large share of the Run, so today's WPM undercounts most on the shortest Stages.
- **Ending on the last keystroke is just as universal.** If a Run keeps counting after the last hit (for example while a clear animation plays), the same bias appears at the end.
- **Idle gaps:** the only documented treatment is Monkeytype's, which treats a whole second with no keypress as AFK. Run Time already leaves pauses out. Whether to also take out within-Run idle time is open. Excluding it would make WPM measure typing pace rather than pace including hesitation. The research standard (first key to last) keeps hesitations in.
- **astro-type's net WPM** is `(correct − incorrect) / 5 / minutes`. Every character needs one correct keystroke, so with accuracy `a` counted over every keystroke, the result is `(2 − 1/a)` times a keybr-style speed:
  - about 5% lower at 95% accuracy;
  - about 11% lower at 90%.

  GNU Typist's adjusted speed would be about 26% lower at 95% accuracy. So astro-type's penalty is mild, and since every Star bar also needs a minimum accuracy, it counts accuracy a second time only a little. If accuracy moves to first attempts, the subtraction needs its own definition: subtract first-attempt errors, or every wrong keystroke?
- **Live paced text:** this one is a question, not a finding. In astro-type the text arrives as Drones. If Drone arrival caps how fast a player can type, WPM partly measures the game's pace. Whatever the bars become, they should sit below the fastest pace the Stage lets a player type. The map's out-of-scope note on Drone pressure is related.

## 3. Typical speeds through about a month of daily practice

### What the sources say

- **No controlled study found.** I found no open-access, primary study that follows people learning touch typing on a full physical keyboard day by day for about a month and reports WPM and accuracy. Two candidates couldn't be checked:
  - Gopher & Raij (1988, IEEE SMC) is cited for novice QWERTY learners over 35 one-hour sessions, but the paper is paywalled and secondary accounts of it disagree.
  - Yechiam et al. (2003, _Human Factors_) trained 22 students on a touch-typing course. The open copy is a scanned PDF with no text layer, so its speed figures couldn't be extracted. Its abstract finds that trainees drift back to looking at the keys because that pays off sooner (["melioration"](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/melioration-and-the-transition-from-touch-typing-training-to-ever/)).
- **keybr.com's own examples** come from self-selected users with a known amount of practice ([help page source](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/page-help/lib/HelpPage.tsx)):
  - "from less than 20 to 40 WPM after 5 hours and 30 minutes of practicing in the course of 11 days";
  - "from 20 to 45 WPM after about 10 hours of practicing in the course of 22 day";
  - "from 30 to 70 WPM after 4 hours 20 minutes … 15 days".

  keybr's default daily goal is 30 minutes ([`settings.ts`](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/keybr-lesson/lib/settings.ts)).
- **keybr's default target** for unlocking each new letter is 35 WPM (175 CPM, see §2).
- **The general population** (Dhakal et al. 2018, results table):
  - mean 51.56 WPM (SD 20.20);
  - fastest tenth above about 78 WPM;
  - slowest tenth below about 26 WPM, with a mean of 20.91;
  - people who said they had taken a typing course averaged 54.35 WPM, against 49.00 for those who hadn't (a small effect, d = 0.27).
- **A job bar:** a US federal clerical posting asks for "40 word per minute or more with 3 or fewer errors based on a 5-minute sample" ([USGS, GS-1106](https://www.usgs.gov/human-capital/procurement-technician-oa-gs-1106)).
- **Typing.com's benchmark bands** run up to 40+ WPM at 98%+ (§1).
- **Researchers' baseline:** Pinet et al. note that touch typists recruited for cognitive studies average 70–80 WPM (citing Logan 2018 and Salthouse 1984).

### Inference

- **Practice time:** P7's 15–20 minutes a day for about a month is 7.5–10 hours. keybr's examples reach 40–45 WPM in 5.5–10 hours, but those learners could already type at 20–30 WPM with some method. A true beginner who has to build every reach from scratch should expect less. **20–25 WPM at 95% after a month is a conservative goal**, matching the slowest tenth of the adult population. It is well short of the 40 WPM job bar and keybr's default target of 35.
- **The early drop:** touch typing is at first slower than looking at the keys (Yechiam et al.), so beginners who already hunt-and-peck at 20+ WPM will at first be _slower_ on touch-typed Stages. Early Stage bars that ask for more than a player's old hunt-and-peck speed reward looking at the keys.
- **Today's bars against the month's goal:** they run well past 20–25 WPM (§5). Through the Sector gates, Stars open Sectors, so these bars decide whether a player on track for the month's goal can progress, not just whether they get a medal.

## 4. A minimum run of clean keystrokes: used anywhere, and why

### What the sources say

- **Not as a pass bar.** None of the tutors' documentation I read uses a minimum run of consecutive correct keystrokes to pass or to earn a Star. That covers Typing.com, TypingClub, GNU Typist, keybr, Ratatype, Typing Agent, Typesy and Monkeytype.
- **Where "Streak" does appear, it counts practice:**
  - TypingClub's badges reward a "5-day typing streak";
  - Typing Agent's "Typing Streaks" add a point per completed lesson or test, and reset after three days without one ([Typing Agent](https://help.typingagent.com/en/articles/2916025-typing-streaks)).
- **The nearest mechanisms all demand zero errors:**
  - Ratatype's target reward "for completing the exercise without errors".
  - TypingClub's five Stars need "the accuracy goal of 100%".
  - Monkeytype's **Master** difficulty "fails if you press a single incorrect key", and **Expert** fails if you submit an incorrect word. Its **stop on error** setting either won't let you continue past a wrong letter, or won't let you move to the next word until it is right ([`metadata.tsx`](https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/blob/master/frontend/src/ts/config/metadata.tsx)).
  - Keyboarding Online's drills can set "Errors Allowed" to 0, and a run that misses any requirement "was not recorded and they will have to redo it". The same page says "in the beginning accuracy is much, much more important than speed" ([Keyboarding Online](https://www.keyboardingonline.com/?p=4167)).
  - GNU Typist's drills don't allow Backspace.
- **The stated purpose,** where one is stated, is putting accuracy ahead of speed: TypingClub's scoring "is optimized to value accuracy over speed", and Keyboarding Online is quoted above.

### Inference

- astro-type's 3-Star minimum Streak is closest to the zero-error rewards: it asks for a stretch of the Run at a local 100%.
- On today's short Stage texts it barely adds anything to the 3-Star accuracy bar. I checked this from the repo, using today's `rules.ts`, where accuracy is rounded to a whole percent and counted over every keystroke. Meeting the 3-Star accuracy bar allows only 2–5 Misses per Run. With those Misses spread as evenly as possible, the longest clean run would still meet the Streak bar on 15 of the 28 Sector Stages. So on those 15 Stages the Streak bar can never fail once the accuracy bar is met. On the other 13 it fails only when the few allowed Misses happen to be spread almost evenly.
- The Streak is also the one Star measure that today's shields can buy (map #123), and the tutors give no support for a clean-run bar beyond "accuracy first". So it is a candidate to drop, or to replace with a zero-error reward such as Ratatype's target. Either is the owner's call.

## 5. astro-type's Star bars today, for reference

From `starThresholds` and `sampleText` in `src/curriculum/curriculum.ts`. A Run is one pass of the Stage's text.

- **1-Star accuracy:** 82–92%, and 85–88% on most Stages (P2 sets a 90% floor; this is a Known departure).
- **2-Star:** 91–97% accuracy, and 10 WPM (Sector 1 Stage 1) rising to 46 WPM (Sector 7 Stage 4).
- **3-Star:** 96–99% accuracy, 14 to 58 WPM, and a minimum Streak of 14 to 50.
- **Stage texts:** 56–196 characters long. At the 3-Star WPM a Run is about 21–66 seconds of typing. On a 56-character text, one Miss is worth about 1.7 accuracy points.
- **Sector gates** (`requiredStarsToUnlock`): 6, 14, 24, 36, 48 and 60 Stars. 2 Stars on every Stage opens Sectors up to 4. Sector 5 then needs 3 Stars on at least 4 earlier Stages, Sector 6 on at least 8, and Sector 7 on at least 12.

**Bars above the month's goal of 25 WPM:**

- **2-Star WPM above 25:** Sector 3 Stages 3–4, all of Sector 4, Sector 5 Stages 3–4, Sector 6 Stages 2–4, and all of Sector 7.
- **3-Star WPM above 25:** every Stage from Sector 2 Stage 3 on.

## Implications for the Star bars (inputs, not decisions)

These are inputs for the owner. None of them changes a rule.

1. **Accuracy floors:** the evidence supports 90 / 95 / 97% as the tutors' common steps, and P2 already sets the floor at 90. On today's measure, Typing.com's defaults (2 Stars at 90%, 3 at 95%) line up with P2 and the month's 95% goal. GNU Typist's 97% is the strictest published pass mark. Bars of 98–99% have no tutor precedent except the "no errors" rewards.
2. **Decide the accuracy measure before setting the numbers.** First attempt per character (keybr, TypingClub's "Real Accuracy") is what P10 asks for. It reads a little higher than today's every-keystroke count for a player who hits several wrong keys in a row, so a bar carried over unchanged becomes a little easier. On short texts, also decide how to round: one Miss is 1–2 points.
3. **The clock:** every documented source starts on the first keystroke and stops on the last. The research standard also leaves out the first character's time. Doing the same in astro-type removes the launch-time undercount on short Stages. Whether to leave out idle gaps (as Monkeytype's AFK seconds do) is a separate choice. No tutor documents doing it inside a lesson.
4. **The net WPM penalty:** today's one-character-per-wrong-keystroke is mild, and published alternatives run from gross (Typing.com) to five characters per error (GNU Typist). Since every Star bar also checks accuracy, gross WPM plus an accuracy bar is the simplest honest pairing. Keeping a net figure measures accuracy twice. Either is defensible.
5. **Speed bars against the month's goal:** the evidence suggests 20–25 WPM after a month is modest but realistic for real beginners. The 2-Star WPM bars from Sector 3 Stage 3 on, and the Sector 6–7 gates' need for many 3-Stars at 28–58 WPM, ask more than that goal. If Stars are meant to track the month's goal, bars near or below about 25 WPM for 2 Stars, with 3 Stars reserved for going beyond, would match. The early Stages also need to allow for touch typing being slower at first than looking at the keys.
6. **The 3-Star Streak:** no tutor uses a minimum clean run to pass. On most of today's Stages the Streak adds little to the accuracy bar, and it is the one bar shields can buy. The options are to drop it, or to replace it with a separate zero-error reward on the Ratatype or TypingClub model.

## What I couldn't find

- **A month-long beginner study:** no open, controlled study of beginners learning touch typing on a full physical keyboard over about a month with daily practice, reporting WPM and accuracy over time. The Gopher & Raij (1988) and Yechiam et al. (2003) figures couldn't be checked against the papers themselves.
- **TypingClub's Star formula:** the WPM and accuracy needed for each of its 1–5 Stars isn't published.
- **Typing Agent's defaults:** its default 1–3 Star targets appear only in screenshots.
- **Typing.com:** its help doesn't say whether corrected errors count toward accuracy, or when its clock starts and stops.
- **Tutors' pacing:** no tutor documents leaving idle gaps out of a lesson's WPM, and no tutor's docs give per-lesson speed goals for the first few weeks.
