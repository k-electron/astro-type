# How established tutors order and size their steps

Research for [How established tutors order and size their steps](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/issues/176), under the map [Phase 3: a curriculum that teaches, with practice that fits the player](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/issues/175). Written 2026-11-26.

**Question:** how do established typing tutors order the keyboard and size their steps? In what order do keys come, and how many are new in each lesson? When do Shift and capitals come, compared with numbers and symbols? How does a new key move from drill to pattern to real words? How many lessons does a whole course have, and how long does each run? Where do review lessons sit? And what does research say about that order (P12)?

**How much to trust each source:**

## Bottom line

1. **Almost every tutor starts on the home row, then takes one of three orders.** keybr is the exception: by default it starts on the six most frequent letters (E N I T R L).
   - **Row by row** (home, top, bottom), filling each row from the index fingers outward: TypingClub, Klavaro, GNU Typist's T and V series, and astro-type today.
   - **Frequency order**, with the most useful letters first so that real words come sooner: keybr (strictly by English letter frequency), TIPP10, GNU Typist's quick Q series and KTouch.
   - **Finger by finger**, from the index fingers outward and across the rows: Typing.com (J F, then U R K, then D E I…) and Ratatype's beginner course (F J, then R T, then U Y…).

   The research finds **no order better than another**. Its one requirement is that the first keys allow real words and sentences at once (West 1974).
2. **The usual step is two new keys.** TypingClub and Klavaro make them a mirror pair, the same finger on each hand (F J, D K, S L, then R U, E I, W O…). TIPP10 and KTouch pick two frequent letters instead (e o, t i, n r). Typing.com and Ratatype's beginner course step two or three keys at a time. GNU Typist steps 2–10 keys a lesson. keybr adds one letter at a time, but only after starting with six. astro-type today adds 2–4 keys a Stage.
3. **Tutors that teach Shift and numbers both put Shift first, with one exception.**
   - **After all the letters:** TypingClub (Shift at lesson 138, numbers at 245) and Typing.com ("Shift Key and Capitalization" is the first lesson after the letters; numbers come four lessons later).
   - **Long before the letters are done:** GNU Typist (lesson 1 of Q, lesson 3 of T, lesson 6 of V) and TIPP10 (lesson 6 of 20, numbers at lesson 16).
   - **The exception:** Ratatype's 15-lesson course teaches the number row before Shift.
   - **No lesson on capitals at all:** Klavaro's basic course and keybr. In keybr, capitals are an optional setting.

   No study I found compares early and late Shift. West's only advice is to practise it in sentences loaded with capitals.
4. **Numbers come after the letters, and symbols come last, everywhere.**
   - **Numbers are taught from the index fingers outward** (TypingClub: 4 and 7 first; GNU Typist V: 1, 4, 5, 6, 7 first; Klavaro: 1–4, then 7–0). West cites studies finding that the order of the numbers made no difference, and that teaching numbers with the letters or later gave equal results (both **secondary**).
   - **Symbols are split into a common set and a rare one,** and the rare set comes last: TypingClub's "Symbols" and "More Symbols", GNU Typist T14–T16, TIPP10's "Special Chars 1" and "2".
   - **Numbers need practice throughout the course.** West says number keys are the most underlearned on the keyboard because they are underpractised.
5. **Drill to pattern to words happens within one lesson, not across many.**
   - **GNU Typist's first lesson** goes: one key alone (`ffff`), then mixed pairs (`fjf jfj`), then the home row (`asdf jkl;`), then words (`sad add all`), then a sentence. Every later Q lesson repeats one shape: rhythm drill, Letter-pair drill, sentences, review line.
   - **TIPP10 and keybr skip the drill:** TIPP10 gives real words from lesson 1, and keybr gives pronounceable made-up words.
   - **Klavaro's basic course is the opposite:** it is all random letter groups. Words come only in its later exercises.
   - **The research** is clear and old. A few seconds of `frf` is fine, but as the main way to teach the keyboard, nonsense drill was "less effective than the immediate use of dictionary words, phrases, and sentences" in every study of it (West 1974).
6. **Course size varies enormously, and so does the share spent on the letters.** It runs from about a sixth of the course (TypingClub: 88 of 684 lessons, 6 of 36 weeks) to over half the lesson list (Typing.com 16 of 29, GNU Typist T 9 of 16, TIPP10 14 of 20, Klavaro 31 of 43).
   - **Lesson counts:** GNU Typist's quick course is 5 lessons, Ratatype's 15 and 19, TIPP10's 20, Typing.com's 29 (with 259 activities), Klavaro's 43, and TypingClub's 684.
   - **Lesson length,** where set: keybr about 100 letters, Klavaro up to 360 characters, KTouch about 2,000, TIPP10 5 minutes.
   - **astro-type's Stage texts** (56–196 characters) are keybr-sized.
   - **The research view:** the letters should be "presented" quickly (West: 4–5 class periods for a good class, at most about 15), because there are hundreds of reaches to learn, not 26. Mastery comes afterwards, from practice on varied real text.
7. **Review sits in three places:**
   - **after every few new-key lessons:**
     - Typing.com: a review after 8–12 new keys;
     - Klavaro: a mixed lesson after every one or two new pairs;
     - Ratatype: four "Repetition" lessons after the letters;
   - **at the end of a section:** Typing.com's "Wrap-up", GNU Typist's "Rhythmic Review", TIPP10's "All Characters", and the review weeks in TypingClub's plan;
   - **inside each lesson:** West recommends opening each lesson with all keys taught so far and closing with test lines that use them all.

   TypingClub and Typing.com also place an adaptive practice lesson at the end of a section.

_Inference:_ the Vision's order (home row, top row, bottom row, capitals with opposite-hand Shift, fluency, numbers, symbols) is TypingClub's order almost exactly, and Typing.com's apart from its letter order. It is within the tutors' consensus, and the research gives no reason to prefer another. The open choices are inside the rows: how soon real words arrive, how many keys a step adds, and how often review comes. The questions are listed at the end.

## 1. The order keys come in, and how many are new each time

### What the sources say

**keybr.com** (open source):

- **The default order is English letter frequency.** The course starts with "a small set of the most common letters … such as ‘E’, ‘N’, ‘I’, ‘T’, ‘R’ and ‘L’", then adds ‘S’, and so on ([help page](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/page-help/lib/HelpPage.tsx)). In code, the letters are sorted by frequency unless the `keyboardOrder` setting is on, and that setting is off by default ([`guided.ts`](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/keybr-lesson/lib/guided.ts#L134-L139), [`settings.ts`](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/keybr-lesson/lib/settings.ts#L17)).
  - The help page's own frequency table gives the full order as e n i t r l s a u o d y c h g m p b k w v f z x q j ([`english.ts`](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/page-help/lib/english.ts)). The live course reads its frequencies from a language model file, so treat this full order as approximate past the first seven.
- **A "keyboard order" option:** with that setting on, home-row letters come first, then top-row letters, then everything else, each group by frequency. The code gives a home-row key weight 1, a top-row key weight 2, and every other key weight 1000 ([`keyboard.ts`](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/keybr-keyboard/lib/keyboard.ts#L123-L145)).
- **How many keys are new at a time:** the course starts with `minSize = 6` letters ([`guided.ts`](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/keybr-lesson/lib/guided.ts#L47)). After that, it adds **one** letter at a time, and only when every letter already included has once reached the target speed (35 WPM by default; see [Accuracy and speed benchmarks](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/blob/main/docs/research/beginner-typing-benchmarks.md), §2) ([`guided.ts`](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/keybr-lesson/lib/guided.ts#L86)). The newest letter appears in every generated word.

**TypingClub** ([_Typing Jungle Teacher Handbook_](https://login.typingclub.com/m/edclubdocs/media/pdf/typing-jungle-handbook.pdf), its default lesson plan):

- **The order is row by row, in mirror pairs.** Its 36-week syllabus (handbook pp. 24–27) gives each week's lessons and new keys. It lists them in what reads as pair order, and the handbook's sample lesson, "Keys r & u", confirms the pairing:

  | :------ | :---------- | :-------------- |
  | 1–11    | Home Row    | f j k d s l     |
  | 12–23   | Home Row    | a ; g h         |
  | 24–38   | Top Row     | r u e i w o     |
  | 39–51   | Top Row     | q y p t         |
  | 52–68   | Bottom Row  | v m c ,         |
  | 69–88   | Bottom Row  | z / b n         |
  | 89–126  | Basic Level 1 (lowercase sentences) | — |

  The sample lesson is "24: Keys r & u" (p. 4).
- **One pair at a time:** "Students should complete the lessons in order. Each pair of characters should be mastered before moving on to the next one" (p. 150).
- **Reaches go along the diagonals:** the top-row section makes "each finger travel diagonally upwards to cover its upper counterpart. After a key on the top row is pressed, all fingers must return to the home row" (p. 14).
- **About three lessons per new key:** 30 keys are taught over lessons 1–88. Not every lesson brings a key. Lesson types include a score-free "Key Introduction", "Character Review", "Practice", "Anchoring" (one hand held on F or J), "Travel" (diagonals), games and videos (pp. 5–12).

**Research** (P12):

- **The order:** "There is no particular order of presentation of keys that has been shown to be most advantageous (by horizontal rows, by diagonal rows, skip-around). The only requirement is that from the first lesson and continuously thereafter, the keys selected permit the immediate use of dictionary words, phrases, or sentences." ([West 1974](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED107884.pdf), "Order of Presentation"). The 1971 review says the same: "all orders are equally effective provided they quickly permit the typing of regular language, rather than nonsense, materials" ([West 1971](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED051377.pdf), p. 6).
- **What makes a first key set easy:** "Starting with words and sentences using only the home row keys or with words and sentences using the stronger fingers will be easier for the student than immediately ranging all over the keyboard and should therefore have good effects on motivation" ([West 1962](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED018630.pdf), p. 7).
- **What to put next to a new key:** the best materials deliberately put each new letter next to each previously taught letter that occurs beside it in the language (West 1974, "Practice Materials"; the scan of this page is partly garbled, so this is a close paraphrase). The point is that the unit of keyboard learning is the letter sequence, not the isolated letter.
- **A caveat on the evidence:** I found no controlled study of key order from after the typewriter era. Recent work, such as the studies cited in the two earlier notes, measures how people type, not the order in which they were taught.

### Inference

- **Three orders, all inside the evidence.**
  - **Row by row** (TypingClub, Klavaro, GNU Typist V, astro-type) maps neatly onto Sectors and onto finger-and-row drills. That suits the map's "drills built around fingers" aim.
  - **Frequency order** (keybr, TIPP10, GNU Typist Q, KTouch) reaches real words sooner, which is the research's one requirement and P8's "real words as soon as possible".
  - **Finger by finger** (Typing.com, Ratatype's beginner course) sits between them: each lesson is one finger's reaches, so vowels such as E and I come early.
  - **The cost of rows:** the home row has one vowel (a), so the first Sector's words are few (`add all ask dad fall flask glass half lad salad`). TypingClub leaves e and i until lesson 24.
- **A middle way already exists in the sources.** GNU Typist's Q series teaches the home row plus E and I in its first lesson. TIPP10 adds E and O straight after the home row. Typing.com teaches E and I by its third key lesson. Bringing E and I, the middle-finger reaches straight up from D and K, forward to the end of the home-row Sector would keep the row-by-row shape and open far more words.
- **Two new keys is the common step size, and a mirror pair is the row-by-row tutors' way of choosing them.** One per hand on the same finger, as in F J, D K, R U and E I. It suits P4's "one small new thing": one new reach, made by both hands. keybr's one-at-a-time step relies on its adaptive speed gate and its generated text. In a library of pre-built Stages, two keys a step keeps the library to a manageable number of steps.
- **West's "letter sequence" rule is a check a library Stage could pass or fail.** It asks for every common Letter pair between the new key and the keys already taught. It fits the Vision's automated checks, and the Logbook already keeps Letter pairs.

**Opposite-hand Shift, where a tutor says which Shift:**

- **TypingClub** "encourages students to use the opposite shift key from the hand being used to type the character", so that they keep their finger placement and return to the home row (p. 16).
- **GNU Typist** T3 says: "Whenever you want to capitalize a key on the left side, you must use the right shift-key", and drills it with `;A; ;F;` before words ([`t.typ`](https://github.com/alexei-matveev/gtypist/blob/master/lessons/t.typ)). V says: "Hold down the left shift key for typing right-hand capitals; hold down the right shift key for typing left-hand capitals" ([`v.typ`](https://github.com/alexei-matveev/gtypist/blob/master/lessons/v.typ)). V6 drills a capital, then the capital plus a letter, then a word: `F Fa Fay`, `J Ja Jay`.
- **Ratatype and Typing.com** don't say which Shift, in what I read. Ratatype warns against Caps Lock.
- **How to teach Shift belongs to [#181](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/issues/181).**

**Research:**

- **Shift:** West gives no evidence on when to teach it. His advice is materials. Early on, use capitals followed by a letter that the Shift finger itself types, such as Pat or Jan after the left Shift and April after the right Shift, so the little finger has to come straight back home. For first teaching, and for "flying capitals", use "sentences loaded with capital letters" (West 1974, "Practice Materials" and "Useful Specialized Accuracy Procedures").
- **Numbers:** "it makes no difference in what order the numbers are presented: whether serially or in random order (Jones, 1966); equal outcomes followed teaching the numbers with the alphabet keys as compared to later teaching of the number keys (Johnson, 1971)" (West 1974, "Learning the Numbers and Symbols"; both studies **secondary**).
- **How often numbers come up:** in one study of employers' documents, numbers were "one eighth of the words" in tables and about 7% in letters, memos and manuscripts. Symbols were an estimated 5% or so of strokes, as far as the scan can be read (West 1974, footnote; the study itself is **secondary**).
- **Numbers need practice throughout:** "The most prominent cause of reported difficulty with top-row keys is that they tend to be underlearned, because underpracticed. Accordingly, reasonable mastery over top-row keys … requires frequent practice on copy including these keys throughout the entire course of learning" (West 1962, pp. 10–11). West meant the number row by "top-row keys".

### Inference

- **Capitals before numbers is the majority view** among tutors that teach both (TypingClub, Typing.com, GNU Typist T and V, TIPP10). Only Ratatype's 15-lesson course reverses it. The research is silent on timing, so the Known departure "Capitals come after numbers" rests on the consensus, not on a study. P12 accepts that.
- **"Right after the letters" (TypingClub, Typing.com) and "right after the home row" (GNU Typist, TIPP10) both have precedent.** The Vision's "after the bottom row" is the TypingClub and Typing.com choice. TypingClub's way of teaching Shift fits a row-by-row path: one walk back over the keyboard in capitals, row by row, in the order the letters were learned.
- **Capitals are far more common than digits in ordinary prose,** and West's figures put numbers at a few per cent even in office documents. Fluency practice on real sentences needs capitals much earlier than it needs numbers. That supports the Vision's order.
- **Numbers and symbols need recurring practice after their own step,** not only a block. TypingClub keeps numbers in its later "Basic Level 3" sentences. This matches the map's reinforcement loops and P6's warm-up.

## 3. How a new key moves from drill to pattern to real words

### What the sources say

- **GNU Typist, inside one lesson.**
  - **T1** goes `ffff`, then `fff fff fff`, then `fff jjj fff jj ff`, then `ffjj jjff fjf fjf jfj jfj`, and so on for each home-row pair. Then `asdf jkl;`, then "Before, we finish, let's try some words" (`sad add all; alas flask fad`), then a sentence (`ask a sad lad`) ([`t.typ`](https://github.com/alexei-matveev/gtypist/blob/master/lessons/t.typ)).
  - **Every Q lesson after the first** has the same four parts:
    1. "Rhythm Drill": reaches such as `deki frju dck, dcl.`;
    2. "Balanced Keyboard Drill": two-letter pairs such as `as os es us is an on en`;
    3. several "Continuous Copy" sentences;
    4. one "Rhythmic Review" sentence

    ([`q.typ`](https://github.com/alexei-matveev/gtypist/blob/master/lessons/q.typ)).
  - **A drill line repeats until it is typed without a mistake.** Q1 says: "If you made no mistakes on the line, I will display the next drill pattern. If you did make a mistake, I will … make you do the line again."
- **TypingClub, across lessons.**
  - **A score-free "Key Introduction" lesson** comes first for each new key, with "written instructions on the correct finger placement" (handbook p. 8). Practice lessons follow.
  - **"Common Patterns" lessons** drill the most frequent letter combinations "in two manners: as stand alone letter combinations and used in words" (p. 17). They come in three sections, after Shift, after numbers and after symbols. Examples: the, ing, tion, est (p. 65); eal, ate, ound (p. 81); own, ill, ity (p. 93).
- **Ratatype:** each new-key exercise is "repeated learning keys and short words containing them" ([advanced curriculum](https://www.ratatype.com/faq/ratatype-curriculum/)). The beginner course moves from keys (lessons 1–12) to "common combinations", then function words, then frequent words, then sentences (lessons 13–19).
- **TIPP10: real words from lesson 1, no drill.**
  - **Lesson 1's texts** are words such as `skald salad falls flask`, after one text that is the key list itself (`asdf jkl;`).
  - **Short words until lesson 6, sentences from lesson 7 on:** the texts average 4–7 characters through lesson 6. The code switches to sentences at `BORDER_LESSON_IS_SENTENCE 7` ([`defines.h`](https://github.com/KarlZeilhofer/Tipp10/blob/master/def/defines.h)).
  - **Lesson 18 draws its texts from lessons 7–18** ([`trainingsql.cpp`](https://github.com/KarlZeilhofer/Tipp10/blob/master/sql/trainingsql.cpp)).
- **keybr: pronounceable made-up words from the start,** "random but readable and pronounceable words, using the phonetic rules of your native language … Typing sensible text is much easier than repeating random letters, and it helps you remember frequent key combinations" ([help page](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com/blob/master/packages/page-help/lib/HelpPage.tsx)). The "natural words" setting, on by default, mixes in real dictionary words that use only the included letters.
- **Klavaro: random letter groups only in the basic course.**
  - **Each lesson** is up to eight lines of nine five-character groups, drawn at random from the lesson's keys ([`basic.c`](https://sourceforge.net/p/klavaro/code/HEAD/tree/trunk/src/basic.c)).
  - **Words, sentences and paragraphs come later,** in separate exercise types: "adaptability" for problem keys, "velocity" for dictionary words, and "fluidness" for paragraphs ([`tutor.c`](https://sourceforge.net/p/klavaro/code/HEAD/tree/trunk/src/tutor.c)).
  - **The message after lesson 43** reads "Positions of keys seems to be learned! … Go to the next type of exercise: adaptability."
- **KTouch: drills, then words.** Lessons 1–3 are pure drills (`ff jjj jf jfj`). Lesson 4 onward is words built from the keys so far.

**Research:**

- **Nonsense drill:** "For the first few seconds of practice at each new reach there is no objection to nonsense drill of the frf variety. However, as a basic medium for teaching the keyboard, nonsense drill has been shown in all the studies of it to be less effective than the immediate use of dictionary words, phrases, and sentences" (West 1974, "Practice Materials"). West's footnote adds that old studies of "all-in-one-lesson using prose materials" against "many weeks using nonsense drill" favoured the former (**secondary**).
- **Repetition:** "practice should be extensive rather than intensive. For example, better one or two tries at each of ten sentences, say, than four attempts at each of five sentences" (West 1962, p. 5).
- **Chaining:** chaining is mostly two- or three-letter sequences. A "40-wpm. typist performs mainly on a letter-by-letter basis" (West 1962, pp. 3–4). So practice should use "a wide vocabulary, in which words occur with their normal frequency", not a small set of common words.

### Inference

- **P8's shape is right; the research shortens its first part.** Every tutor that drills a new key moves to Letter pairs and words within the same lesson. The research says the drill should last "a few seconds" per new reach. P8's "until the reach is automatic" is the only place the principle and the research may pull apart. It is a question for [#182](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/issues/182) and the owner, not a rule change here.
- **A step could be a short ladder of Stages:** an introduction Stage (`fff jjj fjf`, a few seconds), a Letter-pair Stage, then word Stages. GNU Typist's lesson shape (drill, pairs, sentences, review line) is one ready-made template for what a library Stage, or a sequence of them, holds.
- **"Extensive rather than intensive" supports the Vision's large library.** Replaying a step should bring new words, not the same text again. West's case is transfer to new letter sequences, which is a reason beyond memorisation.

**Research on how fast to present the keyboard:**

- **The range:** "All 26 alphabet keys have been successfully 'presented' (which does not mean 'mastered') in one lesson. At the other extreme, three, four and even more weeks are sometimes devoted to alphabet key presentation. The long-drawn-out presentations, however, are based on a devastating misconception of the true meaning of keyboard learning."
- **The reason:** "For the 26 letters of the alphabet there are not 26 responses to be learned, but hundreds", because the reach to r in _from_, _cream_, _erase_ and _brought_ is a different motion each time.
- **The recommendation:** "'Present' the alphabet keys relatively rapidly and let the ensuing weeks take over their proper objective of having the keyboard 'sink in.' Roughly estimated 4.5 days for a good class, 9-10 days for an average group, and not more than about 15 lessons for slow learners", assuming 40–50 minute lessons.
- **The evidence behind it:** West says there has been "no research on rate of keyboard presentation", apart from the old all-in-one-lesson studies. So this is his inference from the research, not a tested result.

(All from West 1974, "Rate of Presentation"; West 1971, p. 6, gives the textbook range as "about five to about fifteen lessons (days) for the alphabet keys".)

### Inference

- **Fit to astro-type's month.** P7's 15–20 minutes a day for about a month is 7.5–10 hours (see [Accuracy and speed benchmarks](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/blob/main/docs/research/beginner-typing-benchmarks.md), §3). Measured in time, the letters take:
  - TypingClub: 4.5–6 hours, 6 weeks at 45–60 minutes a week;
  - West: about 3.5 hours for a good class, about 7 hours for an average one, and up to about 12 hours for slow learners;
  - TIPP10: about 70 minutes (14 lessons at its 5-minute default); Klavaro: about 3 hours at 10 WPM (31 lessons of 180–360 characters). Both assume each lesson is played once.

  If astro-type follows the research and keeps the letters to roughly the first third of the month (2.5–3.5 hours), that leaves about two thirds for capitals, fluency, numbers and symbols, and for practice. At a Run of one to three minutes, that is somewhere around 50–150 Runs for the letters. With two keys a step, the letters are about 15 steps, so a step gets about 3–10 Runs: several Stages, not one.
- **A Stage is keybr-sized,** about 100–200 characters, which is one to four minutes at 10–20 WPM. Klavaro's 360 characters and TIPP10's 5 minutes are longer units. The tutors' lessons are larger than a Stage, so a "step" maps to several Runs, as above.
- **Course length doesn't transfer.** TypingClub's 684 lessons fill a 36-week plan of an hour a week (about 36 hours). Typing.com's fill a school year at 45 minutes a week (about 27 hours, taking a school year as 36 weeks). astro-type has a quarter to a third of that time. Its whole path is closer in size to GNU Typist T (16 lessons) or TIPP10 (20) as a list of steps, with the library and the loops supplying the volume.

## 5. Where review lessons sit

### What the sources say

- **Typing.com:**
  - **"Beginner Review 1"** comes after four key lessons (12 keys: J F Space U R K D E I C G N);
  - **"Beginner Review 2"** comes after the next four (T S L O B A V H M . ,);
  - **"Beginner Wrap-up"** comes after the last three, followed by "Personalized Practice" and "Assessment 1";
  - **the second unit** ends with "Final Review" and "Assessment 2" ([curriculum page](https://www.typing.com/curriculum/keyboarding)).
- **TypingClub:**
  - **"Character Review"** is its own lesson type (handbook p. 5);
  - **"Review" weeks:** the 72-week version of its plan puts a review week after each row and after each Shift section, plus "Lowercase Checkpoint" and "Capital Checkpoint" weeks (pp. 30–34);
  - **"Dynamic Lessons"**, generated from "each student’s weaknesses", appear "at the end of advanced section", and students with all 5 stars on a section are pointed to them (pp. 8, 46).
- **Klavaro:** the consolidation lessons described in §1. After every one or two new pairs comes a lesson on all the keys of that half-row, then the whole row. Lesson 31 is all letters, lesson 36 letters and numbers.
- **GNU Typist:**
  - **each Q lesson** ends with a "Rhythmic Review" line;
  - **T1** reviews within the lesson ("That's enough keys for today. Let's review them.");
  - **V19** is "Practise";
  - **the R series** opens each lesson with a "Warming-Up Drill".
- **TIPP10:** lesson 18, "All Characters", draws from the texts of lessons 7–18. Its "intelligent" mode re-weights every lesson towards the player's error keys (see [What adaptive typing tutors read](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/blob/research/adaptive-practice-data/docs/research/adaptive-practice-data.md), §1).
- **Ratatype (15 lessons):** lessons 5–8 are "Repetition". Their first 12 exercises are each "one word, which should be typed several times", and the last 5 reuse those words.
- **Research:** each keyboard lesson should open with a review of everything taught so far and close with test lines that contain all the letters taught to date (West 1974, "Practice Materials"; the scan is partly garbled, so this is a paraphrase). Straight after the alphabet, learners should practise materials with "a high concentration or density of adjacent-key strokes", such as r against t, m against n, o against i and e against i (West 1962, "Minimizing Substitution Errors", p. 16). Grudin's Mix-up data (beginners' Mix-ups are mostly neighbouring and mirror keys, see [What adaptive typing tutors read](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/blob/research/adaptive-practice-data/docs/research/adaptive-practice-data.md), §4) is the modern evidence for which pairs those are.

### Inference

- **Review comes every 8–12 new keys at most.** Typing.com reviews after 8–12, Klavaro after 2–4, TypingClub after each row. With two keys a step, that is a review step after every two to four steps, or at least one per row.
- **Review at the start of each Stage is cheap, and the research favours it.** A step's Stages could open with keys already learned and end on a line that mixes all of them. The library's tags could ask for that.
- **A Mix-up review built from neighbouring and mirror keys,** after the letters (West's timing) or after each row, is what West recommends and what the Logbook already measures. It is a natural first job for the orchestrator's reinforcement loops: TypingClub's Dynamic Lessons and Typing.com's Personalized Practice sit exactly there.

## Questions for the decision tickets (inputs, not decisions)

1. **Rows, frequency or finger by finger inside the Sectors?** Row by row is the Vision's order and fits Sectors and finger drills. Frequency, or Typing.com's finger-by-finger order, gives real words sooner, the research's one requirement. One option between them is a row-by-row path that brings E and I forward into the home-row Sector, as GNU Typist's Q series does (TIPP10 does the same with E and O). Which one?
2. **Step size:** is a step one mirror pair, two keys on the same finger of each hand? That is the most common choice. Or does it vary, for example three keys for Typing.com-style groups and one for Shift?
3. **Where capitals go:** after the bottom row, as the Vision says, matching TypingClub and Typing.com? And is it taught as one walk back over the rows in capitals, as TypingClub does? The research doesn't decide this.
4. **Drill length:** how long does a new key stay in drill before Letter pairs and words? The research says a few seconds; P8 says until the reach is automatic. This is for [#182](https://github.com/k-electron/astro-type/issues/182) and the owner.
5. **Review placement:** a review step every two to four steps, or once per row? Does every Stage open with known keys and close on a mixed line (West)?
6. **Numbers and symbols:**
   - Numbers from the index fingers outward (4 and 7, or 1 and 4 to 7, first)?
   - Symbols in two sets, common then rare?
   - How do both keep coming back after their own Sector, since West found number keys underlearned because underpractised?
7. **Pace against the month:** how much of the 7.5–10 hours do the letters get? The research's "present quickly" suggests roughly the first third. That sets how many Runs a step has, and so how large the library must be.
