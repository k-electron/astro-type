astro-type · What the Debrief shows of a Run

Debrief Flight Log Prototype

Version D, "Together", combines the three earlier versions and links them. A beginner a few weeks in (about 12 WPM) types a real Free Flight, then the video shows how the parts of the Debrief point at each other. Every number on screen was recorded by the game from those keystrokes.

CThe text, on top

The Run's own words, each letter shaded by how long it took against the player's average, with misses underlined. It is what the player remembers typing, so it leads.

AThe strips, under it

Speed, accuracy, Rhythm and Streak on one time axis. Moving over a strip puts a cursor on all four and outlines the letter typed at that moment; moving over a letter puts the cursor on the strips.

BThe keyboard, at the bottom

Keys tinted by speed with red rings for misses, beside short lists of slowest keys, least accurate keys and mix-ups. Hovering or clicking a key lights up every place it was typed in the text and dims the rest.

Do we want a chart library?

I measured each candidate as the Debrief would use it, minified and gzipped. Today the game's main script is about 268 KB gzipped.

OptionAdds (gzip)What it gives
Recharts 3123 KB (+46%)Ready-made charts, tooltips and legends. Restyling it to the game's look and linking it to the text is fighting its defaults.
Chart.js 454 KBCanvas charts with tooltips. Canvas can't be seen by the DOM tests, and linking hovers to React is manual.
uPlot22 KBVery fast canvas time series with synced cursors. The same canvas and styling limits.
visx (scale, shape, axis, tooltip)24 KBReact SVG building blocks: scales, lines, areas, axes with good ticks, tooltip positioning. The look and the linking stay ours.
d3-scale + d3-shape only14 KBJust the maths: scales, nice ticks, smooth and stepped curves. We draw the SVG.

The hard part of D is the linking between text, strips and keyboard, and no library does that for us. A library earns its place in the details: good axis ticks, smooth curves, tooltips that stay on screen. Analytics' journey charts, over weeks and months, will need those more.

Making sense at every level

1 One plain sentence first

The top of the analysis says the single most useful thing about this Run in everyday words, e.g. "You slowed down on a: your left little finger." A beginner can stop there.

2 Pictures before numbers

The text and the keyboard read without knowing any terms: colours, underlines and rings. An intermediate player reads these.

3 Numbers on demand

The strips, milliseconds and counts stay on the page for experts. Each strip says what it means in a hover note, and jargon like "Clean hits" never shows on screen.
